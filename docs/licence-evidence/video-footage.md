# Television footage — licence evidence

`HOUSE-00275`. Feeds phase 21. `cna-house.md` §58.4 already sets the policy: four channels — a
weather map and a clock **generated by our own tooling**, a nature/landscape channel **from CC0
stock footage**, and a static/no-signal channel — and states plainly: *"No broadcast, film or music
content is used."*

So only one channel has an external source, and this task is about that one.

## Three of the four channels need no licence at all

The weather map, the clock and the static/no-signal channel are generated by this project's own
tooling. They carry no third-party rights, need no external source, and are the reason the
television is "legally safe" by construction rather than by diligence. Static is a seeded noise
render; the clock and weather map are draws over our own fonts and our own data.

That leaves the nature/landscape channel.

## Pixabay is not CC0 — verified

Pixabay is the site most often described as CC0, and it has not been for years. Its **own Content
License**, not a Creative Commons one:

> ✓ Use Content for free
> ✓ Use Content without having to attribute the author
> ✓ Modify or adapt Content into new works
>
> **Prohibited Uses:**
> ✕ **You cannot sell or distribute Content (either in digital or physical form) on a Standalone
> basis. Standalone means where no creative effort has been applied to the Content and it remains in
> substantially the same form as it exists on our website.**
> ✕ If Content contains any recognisable trademarks, logos or brands, you cannot use that Content
> for commercial purposes in relation to goods and services. […]
>
> Please be aware that certain Content may be subject to additional intellectual property rights […]
> **It is your responsibility to check** whether you require the consent of a third party […]

Two independent reasons this fails here:

1. **It is not CC0**, and §58.4 asks for CC0 stock footage. A permissive proprietary licence is not
   the same thing, and the difference is exactly what this phase exists to catch.
2. **The Standalone prohibition collides with a published `assets-src/`.** A source clip committed
   there is content distributed "in substantially the same form" with no creative effort applied.
   Transcoding it to a 256 × 144 frame-strip atlas (§58.3) is a transformation, but calling a
   downscale "creative effort" is precisely the arguable reading ADR-0012 forbids relying on.

Note also the last clause: Pixabay itself disclaims responsibility for third-party rights *inside*
the content. That is an uploader-driven platform's chain-of-title problem again, and video is worse
than stills — a clip can contain recognisable people, trademarks and background music.

**Pexels was NOT verified**: `pexels.com/license/` returned **403** to automated retrieval. It is
widely and, on the Pixabay evidence, probably wrongly assumed to be CC0. It must be read before use
and is not approved here.

## What to use instead

**NASA video**, under the Media Usage Guidelines already verified for `HOUSE-00274`. Those
guidelines cover video in the same sentence as imagery — *"NASA content – images, **audio, video**,
and media files … generally are not subject to copyright in the United States"* — with the same
three constraints: no implied endorsement, the insignia is not public domain, and third-party
material on NASA sites is marked. Earth-observation and landscape footage suits a "nature channel"
exactly, and it needs no new verification.

**Or a per-item public-domain archive.** The Internet Archive hosts genuinely public-domain film
(the Prelinger collection among others), but rights are **per item**, and the item's own rights
statement is the evidence — not the collection's reputation.

## Rules for whatever is chosen

1. Read and archive the **per-item** rights statement; a site-wide reputation is not a licence.
2. Record in `SOURCE.md`: item URL, publisher, rights statement verbatim, retrieval date, source
   hash, and the transcode performed.
3. **No recognisable people, no trademarks, no broadcast/film/music content** — §58.4 and
   `cna-house.md` §59.4. A television inside a game showing recognisable footage is the single
   easiest way for this project to acquire a rights problem, which is why the architecture already
   forbade it before this task ran.
4. `video_transcode.py` (`HOUSE-00219`) records both hashes, as for audio.
